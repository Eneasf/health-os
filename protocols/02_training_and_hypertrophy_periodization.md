# Protocol 02: Resistance Periodization & Mechanical Overload

## 1. 16-Week Periodization Model
To balance fast myofibrillar hypertrophy with slower avascular tendon remodeling:

| Phase | Weeks | Target Mode | Mechanism |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Phase 1: Onset** | Weeks 1–4 | **Standard Mode** | Motor unit recruitment, baseline load establishment |
| **Phase 2: Shift** | Weeks 5–8 | **Eccentric Overload** | Heavy negative phase (+20–30% load) for maximal mechanical tension |
| **Phase 3: Grind** | Weeks 9–12 | **Isokinetic Mode** | Auto-regulated velocity, maximal force across entire ROM |
| **Phase 4: Squeeze**| Weeks 13–16| **Adaptive Mode** | Automated drop-sets and metabolic stress exhaustion |
| **Active Deload** | Weeks 6 & 12 | **Standard (-30% volume)** | Connective tissue collagen synthesis & CNS recovery |

## 2. Connective Tissue & Tendon Nutrition
- **Hydrolyzed Collagen (15g) + Vitamin C (50mg):** Taken 45–60 minutes prior to mechanical resistance sessions.
- **Biological Rationale:** Collagen hydrolysate elevates circulating glycine and proline during exercise. Mechanical loading squeezes fluid from avascular tendons; relaxation draws in amino-acid-rich fluid. Vitamin C is mandatory for enzymatic lysyl/prolyl hydroxylase cross-linking.
- **Rest-Day Blood Flow Restriction (BFR):** Very light occlusion sets (e.g. 50 reps @ 20% 1RM) on rest days to flush metabolite-rich blood to tendon insertion sites without muscle damage.
